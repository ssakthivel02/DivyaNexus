# DivyaNexus Deity Editorial Policy

## Purpose

The deity encyclopedia is an orientation and discovery layer. It helps a learner recognise names, visual attributes, traditions, textual pathways, and source boundaries without claiming to replace scripture study, living worship, specialist history, or initiated theological instruction.

## Required separation

Every deity record must distinguish:

1. Name and discovery aliases
2. Editorial summary
3. Tamil editorial summary
4. Iconographic observation
5. Relationship tradition
6. Associated-text pathway
7. Observance context
8. Source reference
9. Confidence
10. Editorial review status

## Prohibited shortcuts

A record must not:

- invent a verse, verse number, translation, inscription, date, temple chronology, or source URL;
- treat every regional form as identical;
- state one school's theology as the universal Hindu position;
- infer ritual instruction from a museum object;
- convert traditional belief into independently verified history;
- provide current festival dates or temple timings without current verification;
- use an attractive image without provenance and usage rights;
- describe generated explanation as scripture or quotation.

## Source hierarchy

Use the most suitable source for the claim:

- Primary text or critical/recognised edition: wording, passage, textual context
- Temple or institutional source: current practice and visitor information
- Museum collection record: object identification, material, provenance, iconography
- Scholarly publication: historical and interpretive context
- Traditional source: school-specific belief or practice, labelled accordingly

No single source type is sufficient for every field.

## Confidence

- **HIGH** — broad identification or iconographic orientation supported by multiple suitable references.
- **MEDIUM** — a useful orientation with meaningful variation or limited source coverage.
- **EDITORIAL-REVIEW** — a draft pathway that must not be treated as publication-ready fact.

## Tamil quality

Tamil content must be reviewed for:

- natural modern readability;
- correct names and respectful register;
- avoidance of machine-like literal translation;
- regional terminology differences;
- line wrapping on 320px and 375px screens;
- separation between translation, summary, and interpretation.

## Review cycle

Each published record requires:

- a stable slug;
- named source references;
- review date;
- confidence label;
- explicit unresolved questions;
- automated route and search coverage;
- re-review when source links, terminology, or application schema changes.
